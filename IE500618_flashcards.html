Readme: what to prioritise for the 15-minute oral exam

The exam. Individual, about 15 minutes, on 19 or 20 November. You get a few conceptual questions from the lectures, activities, assignments and practicals. The focus is why a method is used and how to interpret results. You do not need to memorise definitions, Python code or derivations, and follow-up questions are likely.

TierWhat is in itWhy
CoreSplit and leakage, Preprocessing, Metrics and thresholds, Pipeline and workflowCovers about half of the 30 questions in the question bank, and this is where follow-ups go. Learn these first.
ImportantFoundations, EDA and plots, Regression (RMSE, R-squared), Classification (probability and threshold), plus the basic Pandas cardsEach maps to several bank questions. Know them well but they are more straightforward.
Light touchInterpretation, Course datasets, plus ROC, AUC and cross-validationOne bank question on interpretation. Dataset cards are for borrowing examples. ROC, AUC and cross-validation were taught but are not in the question bank.
LowTraining math, most Pandas syntaxThe exam says no code or derivations. Only keep the idea of predict, measure loss, update.

Suggested order. Core first, then Important, skim Light touch, and leave Low for last. Filter with the priority chips, and combine them with the theme chips.

How to answer. Out loud, in four parts: what it is, why we use it, a course example, and how to read the result or what can go wrong. Use the Teach list view to explain a topic to someone else.

These priorities are my judgement from the exam information and the question bank, not an official list. Nothing is formally excluded. The instructor says SVM, neural networks, ensemble theory, dimensionality reduction and hyperparameter tuning are out of scope unless announced.
